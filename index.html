    Where The Votes Came From

    Israel has no electoral districts. All 120 seats come from one national count, so no region "wins" seats the way a US state wins electors. But the votes still come from somewhere. Each square is one seat's worth of votes . A region's block shows how many seats its voters filled, and for which parties, as if every state's votes poured into one national pot.

          Sources

          Results: Central Elections Committee for the 26th Knesset (votes26.bechirot.gov.il). The Committee blocks automated downloads from cloud servers, so its official results files are read from the unaltered copy republished by Jacob Weinbren's Israel Election Map, checked about every two minutes on election night. Locality positions: Central Bureau of Statistics. Terrain: Mapzen/AWS Terrain Tiles. Oslo areas: OCHA via the Humanitarian Data Exchange. Borders: Natural Earth. Verses: King James Version; biblical names checked against Strong's Hebrew lexicon.

          Seat Method

          Seats use the Bader–Ofer (D'Hondt) method among lists above 3.25%, with the five 2026 surplus-vote agreements: Likud–Religious Zionism, Shas–United Torah Judaism, Yashar–The Democrats, Together–Yisrael Beiteinu and Joint List–Ra'am. Projections before the count ends are not official; the Elections Committee certifies seats on Nov 4.

          Camps And Red Lines

          Camps reflect each party's declared alignment before the vote. Red lines are simplified from public statements in the campaign. Both update after the President's consultations.
